The goal of this training env is to train the G1 to pick up the spatula by the handle with its right hand. The G1 stands fixed base at the table with its left arm
down by its side and its right hand over the table. Touching the blade ends the episode. Lifting the spatula off the table ends the episode successfully.

Scene: the spatula lies flat on the table (natural). The intended pick is a
LEVER: fingers brace against the tabletop and close the claw so the handle
scoops up into the grip (hand-table contact is legal and expected — only
blade contact terminates). Active-press probing verified thumb+finger force
closure on the flat handle (228 N / 296 N simultaneous). Actions are tightly
bounded joint deltas (waist+arm <= 0.2, fingers <= 0.5 rad/step) with firm
hand PD — the ManiSkill pick recipe. Waist frozen.

Best Practices:

    - Start from the nearest WORKING env and change one thing at a time. Novel mechanics
      each breed their own RL exploit; you find out one training run later.
    - Solve the task first: fix everything fixable (base, object pose, scene) and add
      randomization/realism only after a policy demonstrably works.
    - Match episode length to task duration. A 2 second task does not need a 20 second
      episode; short episodes turn over experience faster.
    - Enforce "never do X" with a termination, not by gating every reward on not-X.
      One rule beats reward plumbing.
    - Every reward channel needs a dense path to its first nonzero signal. A term gated on
      contact/success is flat 0 until the policy stumbles into it - bridge the gap with
      dense shaping (reach, close-fingers-near, progress) or a curriculum that makes the
      gate easy early.
    - Keep dense penalties tiny relative to dense income. Large per-step penalties make
      dying cheaper than living and the policy will find the nearest exit.
    - Price terminations consistently: a controllable early exit must cost more than the
      worst case of living out the episode, and pricing one exit cheaper than another just
      reroutes the exploit through it.
    - A success termination's bonus must exceed the dense income it cuts off, or the
      policy learns to hover just short of success and farm shaping forever.
    - Income that can be collected without progress (pay-for-holding, pay-for-being-near)
      is farmable through reset cycles. Prefer penalties (doing nothing = 0) over income.
    - Watch the per-term reward and termination breakdowns from iteration 1. Flat-zero
      channels and a single dominant termination are diagnoses, not noise.
    - Verify geometry with printed positions AND rendered frames before training, then
      bake the measured values into the config. Assumptions about frames, joint
      conventions, and rest poses are usually wrong.
    - Guard every reward against non-finite states - one diverged env can poison the
      whole batch.
    - Know where your framework resolves config references (e.g. entity cfgs resolve only
      when passed as term params). Unresolved defaults fail silently.
    - Measure throughput before committing to a long run; profile the sim settings on a
      short run first. A wrong solver mode or logging cadence can be a 10x tax.
