The goal of this training env is to train the G1 to pick up the spatula by the handle with its right hand. The G1 stands
fixed base at the 83 cm lab table, left arm down by its side, right hand over the table. There is NO success
termination: the task income is the carry-point flow, paid every step to the 5 s timeout.

The intended grip is a CLAW, not a pinch. Fingertips descend to the tabletop on either side of the handle (index+middle
one side, thumb the other), close horizontally, and rake the handle into the hand. The table is the opposing surface.
Fingertip-on-table contact is legal and expected — it is the grasp mechanism. Handle cross-section, measured off the
collision mesh: 17.5 mm wide / 5.8 mm thick at x=0.12, 24.7 mm / 6.7 mm at x=0.18. The TriHand's flat two-pad pinch
floor is ~15 mm, so a claw closing across the WIDTH is in range where a pinch across the thickness is not.

Reward Terms (cfg = g1_spatula_lift_env_cfg.py RewardsCfg, fn = mdp/functions.py) — the published lift recipe,
UNGATED. Every earlier version multiplied these through _opposed_handle_contact (thumb force AND an opposing digit's
force), a PINCH signature the claw may never produce, which made every task term unpayable:
    reach           1 - tanh(worst(palm, 3 tips)->grasp point / 0.4), no contact gate   mdp.fingers_to_handle
    lift            1 while the spatula root is 3 cm above rest — pure height           mdp.object_lifted
    track           height-gated coarse+fine kernels on distance to the carry point     mdp.track_carry_point
    action_rate_l2  smoothness penalty, -1e-4 -> -0.05 at step 6000                     mdp.action_rate_l2_clamped
    joint_vel_l2    velocity penalty, -1e-4 -> -0.02 at step 6000                       mdp.joint_vel_l2_clamped
    blade_penalty   one-time penalty for the blade termination                          mdp.is_terminated_term

Termination Conditions (cfg = TerminationsCfg, fn = mdp/functions.py):
    Hand contact with blade    blade_contact    mdp.blade_contact
    Spatula lost               spatula_dropped  mdp.object_out_of_bound (off the table or out of reach)
    Physics blow-up            robot_exploded   mdp.robot_or_object_state_invalid (joint vel over 2x limit)
    Timeout                    time_out         (5 s episode)

                             centerline and ALL on the same local-y side (index +0.088,
                             middle +0.081, thumb +0.031) — no straddle at all, and the
                             spatula never moves. The approach geometry, not the finger
                             joints, is what puts the hand in the wrong place.
    assets/author_grasp_map.py  caged-start authoring. NOTE its thumb parameterization is 1-D
                                (one frac for all three thumb joints, flex_bound[jid] * frac):
                                a ray through 3-D thumb space anchored at zero, which cannot
                                express an opposition pose. Its zero-straddle search results
                                are an artifact of that parameterization, not evidence that
                                no claw grasp exists.

    A batched claw-grasp search (one candidate per env, thumb joints swept independently)
    is in development on branch wip/grasp-lab-driver. It is NOT on develop: its scripted
    arm driver saturates the relative-position action and diverges during the approach
    phase, and its pass gate accepted the resulting non-finite states as successes.

Best Practices:

    - Start from the nearest WORKING env and change one thing at a time. Novel mechanics
      each breed their own RL exploit; you find out one training run later.
    - Solve the task first: fix everything fixable (base, object pose, scene) and add
      randomization/realism only after a policy demonstrably works.
    - Match episode length to task duration. A 2 second task does not need a 20 second
      episode; short episodes turn over experience faster.
    - Enforce "never do X" with a termination, not by gating every reward on not-X.
      One rule beats reward plumbing. Corollary: make sure the gate you chose is a
      signature the intended solution actually produces.
    - Every reward channel needs a dense path to its first nonzero signal. A term gated on
      contact/success is flat 0 until the policy stumbles into it - bridge the gap with
      dense shaping (reach, close-fingers-near, progress) or a curriculum that makes the
      gate easy early.
    - Keep dense penalties tiny relative to dense income. Large per-step penalties make
      dying cheaper than living and the policy will find the nearest exit.
    - Price terminations consistently: a controllable early exit must cost more than the
      worst case of living out the episode, and pricing one exit cheaper than another just
      reroutes the exploit through it.
    - Income that can be collected without progress (pay-for-holding, pay-for-being-near)
      is farmable through reset cycles. Prefer penalties (doing nothing = 0) over income.
    - Watch the per-term reward and termination breakdowns from iteration 1. Flat-zero
      channels and a single dominant termination are diagnoses, not noise.
    - Search before you train. A scripted existence proof over thousands of parallel envs
      costs ~2 minutes; discovering the same thing from a reward curve costs six hours.
    - Verify a search can represent the answer before running it. A parameterization that
      cannot express the target pose returns zero hits and reads like a negative result.
    - Verify geometry with printed positions AND rendered frames before training, then
      bake the measured values into the config. Assumptions about frames, joint
      conventions, and rest poses are usually wrong.
    - Guard every reward against non-finite states - one diverged env can poison the
      whole batch.
    - Know where your framework resolves config references (e.g. entity cfgs resolve only
      when passed as term params). Unresolved defaults fail silently.
    - Measure throughput before committing to a long run; profile the sim settings on a
      short run first. A wrong solver mode or logging cadence can be a 10x tax.
